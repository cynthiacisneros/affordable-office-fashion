# Building a Work-Top Rotation

Tops are what make the same pair of pants feel like a different outfit. I do not need a huge collection, but I do like having different shapes and levels of structure so I can dress for a meeting, a normal desk day, or a casual Friday.

## The small rotation I would start with

I would begin with:

- one button-down or collared shirt
- one simple blouse
- one fine-gauge knit
- one clean crewneck or mock-neck
- one top with a color or print I really enjoy

This gives me variety without making the closet hard to manage. I would rather have five tops that work with most of my pants than ten tops that each need a special outfit.

## Keep the shapes different

If every top is loose, the whole wardrobe can feel shapeless. If every top is fitted, I may feel uncomfortable or limited with wide-leg pants. I try to mix one relaxed piece with one more structured piece and pay attention to proportions. A fitted knit can balance wide-leg trousers, while a looser blouse can work with straight-leg pants or a pencil skirt.

I also think about layers. A top that looks good under a blazer or cardigan will get more use than one that only works by itself. My [[../styling-tips/layering-for-changing-temperatures|layering plan]] helps me check sleeve length, fabric thickness, and whether the neckline still looks finished under another layer.

## Make color do some of the work

I usually choose two or three easy neutrals, then add one or two colors that fit my style. That makes it easier to use the ideas in [[../styling-tips/color-pairing-for-the-office|Color Pairing for the Office]] without buying a separate top for every outfit.

Before buying, I ask whether the top works with at least two pairs of pants and one layer I already own. If it does, it earns a better place in my [[../styling-tips/building-a-workwear-capsule|workwear capsule]].
