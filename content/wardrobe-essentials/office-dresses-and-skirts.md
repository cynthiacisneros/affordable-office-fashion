# Office Dresses and Skirts

A dress or skirt can add variety to a work wardrobe without requiring a completely different style of dressing. I look for pieces that feel comfortable while sitting, work with more than one shoe, and can change with a cardigan, blazer, belt, or different accessories.

## What makes a dress useful

I like a dress that can stand on its own but does not have to. A simple midi dress can work with flats for a normal day, a blazer for a meeting, or a cardigan when the office is cold. I check the fabric for transparency and wrinkles, and I sit down before deciding whether the length and shape are practical.

A dress is a better budget purchase when I can create at least three versions of it. For example:

1. dress + loafers + structured bag
2. dress + belt + cardigan
3. dress + blazer + pointed flat

That is the same idea I use throughout my [[../styling-tips/building-a-workwear-capsule|workwear capsule]].

## Choosing a skirt

For skirts, I focus on movement and comfort. A straight skirt can look polished with a blouse or knit, while an A-line or pleated skirt gives me more room to move. I pay attention to where the waistband sits and whether the skirt stays in place when I walk.

I also think about the top before buying. A skirt that only works with one blouse is harder to repeat. I want it to work with my [[building-a-work-top-rotation|top rotation]] and at least one layer from [[../styling-tips/layering-for-changing-temperatures|my layering plan]].

## Keep the styling practical

I would rather choose a simple shape and use color, shoes, or accessories to change the mood. The [[../shoes-and-accessories/index|Shoes and Accessories]] page has the pieces I would use to finish these looks without overspending.
