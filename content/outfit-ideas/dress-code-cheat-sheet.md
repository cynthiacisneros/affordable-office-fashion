# Dress-Code Cheat Sheet

Dress codes are not always written clearly, and different workplaces use the same words in different ways. I treat this as a starting point, then pay attention to what people actually wear and what my workplace expects.

## Business professional

For a more formal office, I would start with tailored trousers or a knee-to-midi skirt, a blouse or knit, a blazer, and closed-toe flats or low heels. A simple dress with a structured layer can work too. I keep the colors coordinated and the accessories simple.

## Business casual

Business casual gives me more flexibility. Straight-leg or wide-leg pants, a polished knit, a button-down, cardigan, loafers, flats, and a simple dress are all useful. I focus on clean lines and good fit so comfortable clothes still look intentional.

## Smart casual

In a smart-casual office, dark jeans may work with a blouse, blazer, cardigan, or polished knit. Clean sneakers can work in some workplaces, but I look at the overall outfit and keep the rest of the pieces structured.

## When I am unsure

I would rather be slightly more polished on the first day than too casual. I can remove a blazer or change an accessory if the office is relaxed, but it is harder to make a very casual outfit look formal at the last minute. I also ask whether the outfit works for meetings, commuting, and sitting comfortably.
