# Shoes and Accessories

Shoes and accessories are where I can make a basic office outfit feel more personal without buying a whole new look. At the same time, I still want everything to be practical. If I cannot walk comfortably in a shoe or if a bag does not hold what I actually need, it is probably not going to become part of my regular work wardrobe.

## Shoes I would actually wear to work

### Loafers

Loafers are one of the easiest office shoes because they work with trousers, dresses, skirts, and even dark jeans on a casual day. I like pairs that look structured but still have enough cushioning for walking.

### Ballet flats or pointed flats

A simple flat can make an outfit look polished without adding the discomfort of a heel. I pay attention to the toe box and heel because a shoe that rubs all day is not worth keeping just because it looks good.

### Low heels

For presentations, meetings, or days when I want something dressier, a low block heel is more realistic for me than a very high heel. I still want to be able to move around normally.

### Clean sneakers, when the dress code allows them

In a more casual office, a clean minimal sneaker can work with trousers or a simple dress. I would keep the rest of the outfit more polished so it still feels intentional.

## Accessories that earn their place

### A practical work bag

I prefer a bag that can hold my everyday essentials without looking oversized. Black, brown, tan, burgundy, and navy are easy to repeat, but a colored bag can also become the accent in a mostly neutral wardrobe.

### Simple jewelry

Small hoops, studs, a watch, or a simple necklace can finish an outfit without making it feel overdone. I like accessories that I can leave on all day and wear with several outfits.

### Belts

A belt can change the shape of a dress, trousers, or a long cardigan. It is also an inexpensive way to repeat a color from a bag or shoe.

### Scarves and small seasonal pieces

A scarf, lightweight wrap, or small accessory can bring in a trend without requiring a large purchase. This is usually how I would test a color or print before spending more on it.

## My comfort rule

I try on shoes with the same kind of socks or hosiery I would actually wear to work, and I walk around before deciding. I also think about how long I will be standing, whether I have meetings across a building, and what my commute looks like.

My [[../styling-tips/fit-and-comfort-checklist|Fit and Comfort Checklist]] applies to shoes and accessories too. Something can be attractive and still be a bad purchase if it makes the workday harder.

## How I use accessories without overspending

I would rather own a few accessories that work across several outfits than buy a different item for every look. A black loafer, simple watch, structured tote, and small gold or silver earrings can work with most of the combinations in my [[../outfit-ideas/index|Outfit Ideas]].

When I want something new, I check my [[../budget-friendly-stores/index|Budget-Friendly Stores]] first and look for one item that adds variety to the pieces I already own.
