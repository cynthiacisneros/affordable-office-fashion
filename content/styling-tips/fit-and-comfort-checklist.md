# Fit and Comfort Checklist

Fit is what makes an affordable item look more expensive. I have learned that a low price is not a deal if the waistband digs in, the shoulders pull, or I spend all day adjusting the hem. Before I decide something is “not my style,” I check whether the cut actually works for my body and my normal routine.

## The five-minute try-on

I try things on with the kind of undergarment and shoes I will really wear. Then I sit down, reach forward, walk around, and raise my arms. For pants, I check that the waistband stays flat and the fabric is not pulling. For a blazer, I look at the shoulder seam and make sure the back does not feel tight when I move. For a skirt or dress, I check the length while standing and sitting.

### My quick checklist

- **Shoulders:** The seam should sit close to the shoulder, without sliding down or making a bump.
- **Waist:** I should be able to sit and breathe normally, especially after lunch.
- **Length:** Sleeves and hems should work with the shoes I plan to wear.
- **Fabric:** I look for transparency, scratchiness, and wrinkles that appear immediately.
- **Movement:** I should be able to reach, walk, and use a bag without constantly fixing the garment.

> Comfort is part of looking polished. If I am distracted by my clothes, the outfit is not finished yet.

Good fit does not always mean buying a smaller size. Sometimes the better choice is sizing up and having the waist or hem adjusted. I compare the alteration cost with the price of the garment, then ask myself if I will wear it enough to make that worth it. This keeps my [[building-a-workwear-capsule|capsule]] focused on pieces I will actually use.

Fit also changes how I style an outfit. A relaxed blazer might look better with a tucked top or belt, while a fitted sweater can balance out wider pants.
