# Fit and Comfort Checklist

Fit is what makes an affordable item look expensive. I have learned that a low price is not a deal if the waistband digs in, the shoulders pull, or I spend the whole day adjusting the hem. Before I decide that something is “not my style,” I check whether the cut is actually right for my body and my routine.

## The five-minute try-on

I try on a piece with the type of undergarment and shoes I will really wear. Then I sit down, reach forward, walk a few steps, and raise my arms. For pants, I check that the waistband stays flat and that the fabric is not pulling across the hips. For a blazer, I look at the shoulder seam and make sure I can move without the back feeling tight. For a skirt or dress, I check the hem while standing and sitting.

### My quick checklist

- **Shoulders:** The seam should sit close to the shoulder, without sliding down or making a bump.
- **Waist:** I should be able to sit and breathe normally, especially after lunch.
- **Length:** Sleeves and hems should work with the shoes I plan to wear.
- **Fabric:** I look for transparency, scratchiness, and wrinkles that appear immediately.
- **Movement:** I should be able to reach, walk, and use a bag without constantly fixing the garment.

> Comfort is part of looking polished. If I am distracted by my clothes, the outfit is not finished yet.

Good fit does not always mean a smaller size. Sometimes the best affordable choice is sizing up and having a hem or waist adjusted. I compare the alteration cost with the price of the garment, then decide honestly whether I will wear it enough. This keeps my [[building-a-workwear-capsule|capsule]] focused on pieces that earn their place.

Fit also changes how I style an outfit. A slightly relaxed blazer may need a tucked top or a belt, while a fitted knit may look better with wider pants.
