# Layering for Changing Temperatures

Office temperature is one of the most annoying parts of getting dressed. It can be hot on the walk to work, cold under the air conditioner, and comfortable again by the time I leave. My solution is to build outfits in light layers instead of wearing one heavy item that I cannot adjust.

## Start thin and add structure

I like a breathable base, a light middle layer, and one polished top layer. A fitted tee, sleeveless shell, or thin knit can sit under a button-down, cardigan, or vest. A blazer finishes the outfit when I need more structure. The pieces look intentional when the layers have different lengths or textures, such as a smooth shirt under a soft knit or a cropped jacket over a longer top.

### What I keep at my desk

I keep a neutral cardigan and a blazer nearby. They work with most of my [[building-a-workwear-capsule|capsule pieces]] and make it easy to adjust my outfit for meetings or a cold office. A cardigan is especially useful when I want to stay warm without adding bulk under a coat. If my outfit already has a bright color from [[color-pairing-for-the-office|my color plan]], I choose a quiet layer so the whole look stays balanced.

For a normal workday, I check these details before leaving:

- Can I remove the top layer without ruining the outfit?
- Will the base layer still look finished on its own?
- Are the sleeves comfortable under my coat and blazer?
- Do the fabrics wrinkle badly after I sit for an hour?

#### The small styling trick that helps

I roll or push the sleeves of the outer layer only when the inside layer is neat. Showing a small amount of a cuff or contrasting color makes the outfit look styled rather than accidental. I also use one consistent detail, like gold jewelry or a leather belt, to connect the layers.

Layering should make a workday easier, not create a pile of clothes to carry. For outfits that have to transition after work, I use a removable blazer or cardigan from the [[desk-to-dinner-outfit-formulas|desk-to-dinner formulas]] page and finish by checking movement with the [[fit-and-comfort-checklist|fit checklist]].
