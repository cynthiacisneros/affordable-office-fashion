# Layering for Changing Temperatures

Office temperature is honestly one of the most annoying parts of getting dressed. I can be warm on the way to work, freezing under the air conditioner, and comfortable again when I leave. I deal with it by wearing light layers instead of one heavy piece that I cannot adjust.

## Start thin and add structure

I usually start with a comfortable base, add a light middle layer, and finish with something that gives the outfit shape. A fitted tee, sleeveless top, or thin knit can go under a button-down, cardigan, or vest. I use a blazer when I want the outfit to look a little more polished. Different lengths and textures help too, like a smooth shirt under a soft sweater or a shorter jacket over a longer top.

### What I keep at my desk

I keep a neutral cardigan and a blazer nearby. They work with most of my [[building-a-workwear-capsule|capsule pieces]], and I can grab one quickly if I have a meeting or the office is cold. A cardigan is great when I want to be warm without adding a lot of bulk under my coat. If my outfit already has a bright color from [[color-pairing-for-the-office|my color plan]], I use a neutral layer so everything still looks balanced.

Before I leave for a normal workday, I check a few things:

- Can I remove the top layer without ruining the outfit?
- Will the base layer still look finished on its own?
- Are the sleeves comfortable under my coat and blazer?
- Do the fabrics wrinkle badly after I sit for an hour?

#### The small styling trick that helps

I only roll or push up my outer sleeves when the layer underneath looks neat. Showing a little cuff or a different color makes the outfit look intentional. I also repeat one small detail, like gold jewelry or a leather belt, to make the layers feel connected.

Layering should make my workday easier, not leave me carrying a pile of clothes. If I have plans after work, I use a blazer or cardigan from the [[desk-to-dinner-outfit-formulas|desk-to-dinner formulas]] page and check that I can move comfortably with the [[fit-and-comfort-checklist|fit checklist]].
