# Color Pairing for the Office

Color is my favorite way to make affordable office clothes feel fresh. A basic black trouser can look completely different with a cream knit, a blue button-down, or a red cardigan. I do not try to follow every trend. I choose combinations that look intentional and that I will still want to wear after the first week.

## The three-color rule

When I am in a hurry, I use two quiet colors and one color that does the talking. For example, navy trousers, a white top, and a burgundy bag are polished without being boring. Camel, black, and soft blue is another easy combination. If the clothing already has a strong print, I keep the accessories simple so the outfit has somewhere to rest.

> The easiest outfit to repeat is the one with a clear color story, not the one with the most pieces.

### Affordable ways to test a color

I test a new color in a small item first, such as a scarf, belt, earrings, or knit top. That lets me see whether it works with my existing [[building-a-workwear-capsule|workwear capsule]] before I spend more on a blazer or coat. I also check the color in daylight because store lighting can make a shade look warmer or brighter than it really is.

Here are a few combinations I keep coming back to:

1. Navy + cream + burgundy for presentations or interviews.
2. Chocolate brown + pale blue + gold for a softer everyday look.
3. Gray + black + one bright accent for a modern, minimal outfit.
4. Olive + ivory + tan for a relaxed workplace that still feels pulled together.

Color should support the day, not distract me from it. If I know I will be moving around a lot, I pair the outfit with the shoes from my [[fit-and-comfort-checklist|comfort checklist]]. If the weather is unpredictable, I save the strongest color for a layer that can come off easily, using the ideas in [[layering-for-changing-temperatures|Layering for Changing Temperatures]].
