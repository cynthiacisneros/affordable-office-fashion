# Color Pairing for the Office

Color is one of my favorite ways to make affordable office clothes feel new. The same black pants can look totally different with a cream sweater, a blue button-down, or a burgundy cardigan. I am not trying to copy every trend I see. I just like combinations that look put together and that I will still want to wear after the first week.

## The three-color rule

When I am in a hurry, I use two calm colors and one color that stands out. Navy pants, a white top, and a burgundy bag look polished without feeling boring. Camel, black, and soft blue is another easy combination. If one piece already has a strong print, I keep the accessories simple so the outfit does not feel too busy.

> The easiest outfit to repeat is the one with a clear color plan, not the one with the most pieces.

### Affordable ways to test a color

If I am unsure about a new color, I try it in a small item first, like earrings, a belt, a bag, or a knit top. That helps me see if it works with my [[building-a-workwear-capsule|workwear capsule]] before I spend more on a blazer or coat. I also check the color in daylight because store lighting can make a shade look very different.

These are a few combinations I keep coming back to:

1. Navy + cream + burgundy for presentations or interviews.
2. Chocolate brown + pale blue + gold for a softer everyday look.
3. Gray + black + one bright accent for a modern, minimal outfit.
4. Olive + ivory + tan for a relaxed workplace that still feels pulled together.

Color should make my day easier, not give me one more thing to worry about. If I know I will be walking or standing a lot, I choose shoes from my [[fit-and-comfort-checklist|comfort checklist]]. If the weather is unpredictable, I put the strongest color in a layer I can take off, using the ideas in [[layering-for-changing-temperatures|Layering for Changing Temperatures]].
