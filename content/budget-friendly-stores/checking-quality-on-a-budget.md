# Checking Quality on a Budget

Affordable does not have to mean careless. I try to inspect a piece before buying because the price only matters if the item holds up and stays comfortable enough to wear.

## What I check in the store

I look at the seams first. They should lie flat without loose threads or obvious pulling. I check buttons, zippers, hems, and the inside lining because small construction problems usually become more annoying after the first few wears.

I also hold the fabric up to the light if the piece is supposed to be opaque. Then I move around in it, sit down, and see whether the fabric becomes tight or transparent. My [[../styling-tips/fit-and-comfort-checklist|Fit and Comfort Checklist]] helps me remember the details when I am in a hurry.

## Think about care

A piece that needs dry cleaning after every wear may not be the best budget option for me. I look at the care label and think honestly about whether I will maintain it. I also consider wrinkles, pilling, and whether the color will work across seasons.

For workwear, I usually prefer fabrics that can handle a normal commute and a full day of movement. That does not mean every item needs to be perfect. It means I should know what I am buying and what tradeoffs I am accepting.

## Use cost per wear as a reality check

If a $25 top gets worn once, it was not necessarily a better value than a $60 top that becomes part of my weekly rotation. I do not calculate an exact number every time, but I do ask whether the piece will work with my [[../wardrobe-essentials/choosing-work-pants|work pants]], [[../wardrobe-essentials/building-a-work-top-rotation|top rotation]], and [[../outfit-ideas/index|outfit ideas]].
