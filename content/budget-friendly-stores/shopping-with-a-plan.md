# Shopping With a Plan

Shopping on a budget works better for me when I decide what I need before I start browsing. Otherwise, a sale can make me buy another version of something I already own while the actual gap in my wardrobe stays there.

## My short pre-shopping list

Before I shop, I write down:

- the specific category I need, such as work pants or a light layer
- two or three colors that will work with my current wardrobe
- the dress code or situations I need the piece for
- a comfortable price range
- at least three outfits I want to make with it

This keeps the shopping trip focused without making it feel too strict. I still leave room for something unexpected, but it has to fit into the wardrobe I already have.

## Start with the closet

I look at what I own before I look at a store. I check whether I already have a similar item that only needs an alteration, cleaning, or a different styling idea. Sometimes the answer is not a new purchase. It is a better combination of pieces, like using a belt, blazer, or brighter top.

When I do need something new, I compare options using the stores on my [[index|Budget-Friendly Stores]] page. I also keep my [[../wardrobe-essentials/index|Wardrobe Essentials]] list nearby so I do not mistake a trend for a real need.

## Give the item a job

I ask myself whether I can wear the piece to work, whether it works with clothes I own, and whether I can style it more than one way. If the answer is no, I pass even if the price is low.

The best budget purchases are not always the cheapest. They are the pieces I wear often, feel comfortable in, and can keep using after the season changes. For a closer look at construction and fabric, see [[checking-quality-on-a-budget|Checking Quality on a Budget]].
